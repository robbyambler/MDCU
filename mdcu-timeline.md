# MDCU Personal Chronology

Approximate dates are from an Earth frame of reference. The character's subjective experience stretches across these eras due to long-duration missions and relativistic effects in *Interstellar*.

| Personal era | Approx. era (Earth) | Film / alias | MDCU interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Origin / military guilt | 1991 | *Courage Under Fire* – Specialist Ilario | Young soldier in the Gulf War falsifies a friendly-fire account to protect himself and his unit. Survives the war; his moral bearings do not. The institutional machinery that rewards his silence becomes the first entry in a long catalog of rigged systems. |
| Early legal crusader | Mid-1990s | *The Rainmaker* – Rudy Baylor | Young Memphis lawyer who sues a corrupt insurer over a denied bone marrow transplant. Wins in court. Discovers that winning inside broken rules leaves the rules intact. Leaves law — but doesn't enlist yet. |
| Wounded prodigy in Boston | Late 1990s | *Good Will Hunting* – Will Hunting | Retreats to South Boston under a different identity, living as a janitor and self-taught math prodigy while hiding his Gulf War record and earlier legal career. Leaves not for California but for New York. |
| Underground economy | 1998–2000 | *Rounders* – Mike McDermott | Columbia Law student running underground poker games in New York. Mathematical intuition weaponized at high-stakes card tables. Exposure to organized crime and back-channel dealing draws the attention of a recruiter. |
| Robin Hood period | Late 1990s–early 2000s | *Ocean's Eleven / Twelve / Thirteen* – Linus Caldwell | Joins Danny Ocean's crew as the junior apprentice. Math aptitude and card instincts translate into misdirection, identity performance, and the long con. Three films; grows from rookie to peer. Targets are casinos and corrupt operators — exits clean, rationale Robin Hood. His profile gets noticed by people who aren't casino security. Military recruitment follows. |
| Military crucible | Early 2000s | *Green Zone* – Roy Miller | U.S. Army Chief Warrant Officer in Iraq discovers the WMD intelligence justifying his missions is fabricated. Pushes back without authorization. Gets noticed by CIA talent scouts. |
| Black-ops creation | Early–mid 2000s | *Bourne* series – "Jason Bourne" / David Webb | Recruited into Treadstone; memories stripped and a new persona engineered as a deniable CIA asset. Recovers too much. Dismantles the program across four films. The heist skills from the Ocean's years surface again — this time without the team, without the rationale, and without the exits. |
| Criminal immersion | Mid-2000s | *The Departed* – Colin Sullivan | Long-term deep-cover Boston cop on Frank Costello's payroll. Where the Ocean's years were crime with exits, The Departed has none. Dual loyalties corrode whatever fixed self survived Treadstone. |
| Criminal immersion (concurrent) | Mid-2000s | *The Rip* – [alias classified] | A parallel or adjacent criminal assignment in the same moral register as The Departed. The cover stops being a cover. Both assignments collapse together; he is extracted and rebuilt in the corporate world. |
| Corporate fracture | Late 2000s | *The Informant!* – Mark Whitacre | Agribusiness executive and FBI informant whose accumulated compartmentalized identities manifest as documented paranoia and mania. Federal prison. Reemerges with a clearer self-picture. |
| Global energy cover | Mid–late 2000s | *Syriana* – Bryan Woodman | Geneva-based energy analyst and adviser to a Gulf prince. Son dies at a royal estate. Reform prince is assassinated. He survives, as usual. |
| Going to ground | Late 2000s | *Stillwater* – Bill Baker | Surfaces in Marseille as an Oklahoma roughneck — maximum distance from his Geneva identity. Navigates French bureaucracy and local criminal infrastructure on a personal mission. First time operating from personal stakes since Baylor. |
| Glimpse behind the curtain | Around 2010s | *The Adjustment Bureau* – David Norris | Reinvented as a rising New York politician, he finally sees the Adjustment Bureau — plan editors enforcing a hidden architecture for human history. Refuses the plan. First act in forty years driven purely by personal loyalty. |
| Corporate operative who turns | Early 2010s | *Promised Land* – Steve Butler | Natural gas landman buying fracking rights from farmers across rural America. Charm and patience of twenty years' cover work deployed for a corporation. Turns against it from inside. The Whitacre instinct, with more deliberate execution. |
| Pandemic survivor | Early 2010s | *Contagion* – Mitch Emhoff | Survives a lethal global pandemic with inexplicable immunity. Biological profile and survival record reach NASA mission planning. |
| Escape to space | 2030s | *The Martian* – Mark Watney | NASA astronaut and botanist; stranded on Mars and presumed dead, survives via engineering creativity and rigorous mathematics — the Will Hunting mind, finally unencumbered. |
| Return, guilt, and recruitment | 2040s–2050s | Post-*Martian*, pre-*Interstellar* gap | Returns to a Blight-ravaged Earth, quietly teaches and consults, then volunteers for a one-way Lazarus mission as atonement for past roles in policy and exploration. |
| Exile by wormhole | Late 21st century | *Interstellar* – Dr. Mann | Under the alias Dr. Mann, surveys an ice world through a wormhole. Years of total isolation. He writes heroic, mythic, and absurdist stories about himself — including one about a man who murders someone and steals their identity, written while he is actively planning to falsify his data and steal a rescue ship. Then the Endurance arrives. He falsifies his findings. He tries to take the ship. He dies in the airlock explosion. Bulk beings preserve a thread. |
| Authored works (written during Interstellar isolation) | Concurrent with Mann's ice planet years | *The Great Wall* (William Garin), *Saving Private Ryan* (Pvt. Ryan), *The Talented Mr. Ripley* (Tom Ripley), *All the Pretty Horses* (John Grady Cole), *Dogma* (Loki), *The Good Shepherd* (Edward Wilson), *True Grit* (LaBoeuf), *The Monuments Men* (James Granger), *Suburbicon* (Gardner Lodge), *The Last Duel* (Jean de Carrouges), *Thor: Love and Thunder* (Asgardian actor), *Stuck on You* (Bob Tenor) | Twelve stories written during the years of waiting. Heroic wish-fulfillment, a CIA origin myth written by the CIA's most destroyed product, mythic grandiosity, one absurdist comedy about conjoined twins, and at least one story that reads as direct psychological projection of what he was about to do. |
| Class war endgame | 22nd century | *Elysium* – Max Da Costa | Dying factory worker hacks Elysium's medical system to make it universal. Final act mirrors Baylor's first lawsuit. Different body, different century. The loop closes. |
